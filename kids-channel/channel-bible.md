# Bolt & Mango — Channel Bible

**Platforms:** YouTube Shorts + TikTok (same vertical 9:16 video)
**Audience:** kids 5–8, English
**Style:** bright stylized 3D animation, little dialogue, lots of sound effects
**Length:** 30–50 seconds, built to loop
**Cast lineup image:** Higgsfield job `d8fecbea-0450-491e-91c9-3458c680abbd`

## Cast

| Character | Look | Personality | Role in episodes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bolt** | Tiny blue robot, cyan pixel eyes on a face screen, antenna with a yellow lightning-bolt tip, orange rocket boots | Fast, overconfident, always has a "genius plan" | Starts the plan that goes wrong |
| **Mango** | Big fluffy mango-orange monster, cream belly, small purple horns, one buck tooth, always holding food | Slow, kind, hungry, accidentally wise | Fixes the problem in a simple, kind way |
| **Pickle** | Small lime-green gremlin, huge pointy ears, sly yellow eyes, backwards red cap | Prankster, cheeky, never mean | Rival and chaos-starter; often gets pranked back |

Catchphrases (short, easy to remember):
- Bolt: "Easy-peasy, robot-squeezy!" (just before it goes wrong)
- Mango: "Mmm… *snack time*."
- Pickle: "Gotcha!" (the whoopee cushion sound effect is his signature)

## Repeatable formats (test each, keep what works)

1. **Bolt's Big Plan:** Bolt tries a shortcut (cleaning his room with a rocket, say), it fails in a funny way, Mango shows the simple way. Each one has a quiet lesson.
2. **Pickle's Prank:** Pickle sets up a prank, someone else turns it around, and Pickle gets "gotcha'd" himself.
3. **Mango vs. Food:** a giant sandwich or noodle challenge, with a surprise ending.
4. **What's Inside?:** a mystery box shakes and wobbles, and the reveal comes at the end. Good for loops.
5. **Would You Rather?:** two silly options on screen, kids answer out loud, and the characters react.
6. **Bolt Fact Blast:** one wow fact about animals, space or the body, Bolt-style, in 20 seconds.

## Rules for every Short
- **0–2s hook:** start mid-action (a crash, a scream, a big reveal setup). No intro, no logo.
- Something new on screen every 3–5 seconds. Use pop, whoosh, boing and fart sounds.
- The story must make sense with the sound off: big expressions, one clear goal.
- The last frame matches the first frame, so the video loops.
- One idea per Short. Formats run as series (Part 1, 2, 3).
- Parent-safe: no scary content, no mean bullying, no dangerous stunts kids could copy.
- YouTube: mark the videos **Made for Kids**. TikTok: frame it as family content (TikTok is 13+).
- Use AI as a production tool only. Keep the characters on-model, and give every Short a real story. Don't mass-produce near-identical clips.

## Posting cadence
- Weeks 1–4: 1 Short a day, rotating formats, while you track views and replays.
- After that: 2 a day, focused on the 2 best formats. Later, compile Shorts into 5–8 minute long videos.
